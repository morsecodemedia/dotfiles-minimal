
# Visual Identities

## Overview

The central concept within Image Lab is the **Identity**.

An identity represents a reusable visual concept that persists across projects, generations, and versions.

Images are renderings of an identity.

They are not the identity itself.

---

## Why Identities Exist

Creative work often revisits the same subjects.

Examples include:

* Characters
* Products
* Brands
* Locations
* Creatures
* Vehicles
* Props
* Materials
* Styles
* Color palettes

Without identities, every new project begins from scratch.

Identities provide continuity.

---

## Identity Structure

An identity may include:

* Metadata
* Description
* Reference sets
* Style references
* Preferred workflows
* Generation settings
* Associated models and LoRAs
* Relationships
* Version history

Generated images become part of an identity's history rather than replacing it.

---

## Identity Lifecycle

```text
Exploration
      ↓
Development
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Frozen
      ↓
Versioned
      ↓
Published
      ↓
Referenced
```

Once an identity is frozen, it becomes the canonical reference for future work.

Subsequent changes create new versions rather than replacing previous ones.

---

## Reuse Across Projects

Projects consume identities.

They do not own them.

A single character, brand, or product may appear across dozens of independent projects while maintaining a consistent visual language.

Projects may intentionally reference specific identity versions to preserve continuity.

---

## Relationships

Identities may reference one another.

Examples include:

* Characters belong to worlds.
* Vehicles belong to brands.
* Products belong to companies.
* Materials belong to style libraries.
* Color palettes belong to brand systems.

These relationships create a connected creative ecosystem rather than isolated assets.

---

## Guiding Principle

> Ideas evolve. Identities endure.
